## 2. The Dominance Paradox — The Hinge of Hierarchy

The biological engine creates a real advantage. The individual can hunt better, plan better, predict better. That advantage translates into more resources, more safety, more future time. This is not an illusion; it is a measurable, physical fact.

But then the self‑model expands. The mind observes its own power and asks: *“Why do I have this advantage?”*

The correct answer is: *“Because my biological engine—a temporary, inherited configuration of Y‑units—happens to be running a more efficient cognitive algorithm in this environment.”*

The answer the mind gives is: *“Because I am superior. I am the source of this power. It belongs to my identity.”*

**This is the Dominance Paradox:** the individual correctly observes the advantage, but incorrectly attributes the ultimate source of that advantage entirely to the self. The “I” claims ownership of a biological gift it did not create, and then asserts that ownership as a justification for hierarchy.

## 3. Why Preaching Fails — The Paradox Is the Identity

Now the ethical layer becomes completely transparent.

Every attempt to correct unethical behavior—every sermon, every scripture, every ethical philosophy—argues with the **conclusion** of the paradox (domination, extraction, cruelty). But it does not touch the **premise** of the paradox (the misattribution of the biological edge to the self).

When you tell the Dominant: *“What you are doing is wrong. You should share. You should be compassionate,”* the Dominant does not hear a moral argument. They hear an **attack on the “I.”** Because the “I” has claimed ownership of the advantage, any challenge to the domination subroutine is experienced not as a correction, but as an existential threat.

The Dominant’s firmware requires the misattribution to function. Without it, the entire hierarchy collapses. Without it, the Dominant must confront the terrifying truth: *“I am not the source. I am a temporary arrangement of Y‑units, no more inherently valuable than the serf in my field. My advantage is borrowed, not owned.”*

This is a truth that the Dominant’s entire psychological structure is designed to avoid. So preaching fails—not because the ethical message is wrong, but because it is arguing with a misattribution that the Dominant’s identity depends on.

## 4. The “I/him” Split — The Cognitive Root of Unethical Behavior

The cognitive revolution (roughly 50,000–30,000 years ago) produced the fundamental subject‑object divide: “I am this; he is that.” This was a necessary cognitive tool for modeling reality. But when the resource‑scarcity loop activated, the “I/him” split became the foundation of the ethical gradient.

- **“I” am the centre of the moral universe.** My safety, my children, my tribe—these are primary.
- **“He” is outside the circle.** His safety, his children, his tribe—these are secondary, or irrelevant, or even threatening.

The closer the biological connection, the stronger the ethical obligation. The more distant the connection, the weaker the obligation. This is the **proximity gradient** of ethics, hardwired into the biological engine.

The feudal system exploits this gradient ruthlessly. It keeps the masses at the edge of survival precisely to keep their circle of ethical concern small. A starving parent cannot afford to care about a stranger’s child. A burned‑out professional cannot afford to question the extraction protocol. The poverty sticker is an ethical cage.

The Space State does not argue with the Dominance Paradox. It does not preach. It does not moralize. It **removes the resource‑scarcity loop that powers the paradox.**
