### The Nature of Photon Mass
Mainstream physics correctly states that a photon has zero rest mass. However, describing light as simply "massless" is physically incomplete and misleading. It implies a lack of physical reality. Light possesses nonzero energy and momentum, which grants it a nonzero mass-equivalent. 

The invariant energy-momentum relation is:

$$
E^2 = (pc)^2 + (m_0 c^2)^2
$$

For a photon, the rest mass $m_0 = 0$. Therefore, the equation simplifies to:

$$
E = pc
$$

By mass-energy equivalence, the photon carries a real mass-equivalent:

$$
m_{eq} = \frac{E}{c^2} = \frac{hf}{c^2}
$$

Light is rest-mass-free, but energy-mass-real. This is why light can exert radiation pressure, heat matter, bend in gravity, and increase the total mass of a closed system (e.g., a box filled with light weighs more than an empty box).

### The P+X Sequence of Light Propagation
Light traveling from the Sun to Earth is not a one-time teleportation event. It is a continuous sequence of energy distribution across the Quantum Planum Field (QPF). 

If point A is the Sun and point B is Earth, the path consists of countless intermediate states (A+1, A+2, ..., A+n). At every single point along this trajectory, there is an active electromagnetic field, photon flux, energy, and frequency. Energy did not emerge from zero at point A; it was converted from the Sun's mass-energy reservoir via nuclear fusion. The Sun loses approximately 4.3 million tonnes of mass-equivalent per second to sustain this emission.

### Conservation of Energy
The framework enforces a strict conservation law: nothing comes from zero, and nothing goes to zero. Energy, velocity, momentum, and electromagnetic waves are all manifestations of mass-energy. If light were truly "zero" in all respects, energy would be created from nothing, violating fundamental conservation principles.
